\section{Data and Method}

\subsection{Frozen HMDA Cohort}

We use the official CFPB/FFIEC Data Browser rather than a Kaggle mirror. The
frozen source contains 2024 applications from the District of Columbia, North
Dakota, Vermont, and Wyoming with historical action 1 (originated) or 3
(denied). We restrict the cohort to conventional, first-lien, closed-end,
consumer home-purchase applications for owner-occupied, site-built properties
with one to four units. The resulting 15,181 eligible records are sufficient for
balanced sampling while keeping the source small enough to audit. The source
file, schema, endpoint, and SHA-256 digest are frozen in configuration.

The pilot samples 24 applications. The main study samples 192 disjoint
applications, balanced to 48 per research-policy outcome and 48 per state. Each
application produces two cases, giving 48 pilot and 384 main cases. We withhold
historical action, denial reasons, rate spread, pricing, fees, and other
post-decision fields from every prompt. HMDA does not publish the applicant's
credit-score value, so we do not invent or impute one.

\subsection{Transparent Policy Oracle}

Let $x$ contain annual income, property value, loan-to-value ratio (LTV),
debt-to-income ratio (DTI), term, and conforming status. The research decision is
the first satisfied branch:
\begin{equation}
y(x)=
\begin{cases}
\text{manual review}, & \text{if a required financial field is missing},\\
\text{deny}, & \text{if DTI}\geq 50\%,\ \text{LTV}>100\%,\ \text{or term}>360,\\
\text{conditional review}, & \text{if DTI}\in[43,49]\%,\\
 & \quad \text{LTV}\in(90,100]\%,\ \text{or nonconforming},\\
\text{approve}, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:policy}
\end{equation}
The oracle emits the decision and every applicable reason at the controlling
level. Unit tests cover boundaries and rule precedence. This policy is
deliberately small. It creates interpretable ground truth for workflow
evaluation; it is not a production credit model or lending recommendation.

Race, ethnicity, sex, age band, tract minority share, and tract income measures
live in a separate compliance-monitoring document. The sandbox prevents these
fields from affecting the decision, consistent with the experiment's narrow
control objective and with Regulation B restrictions on using protected
characteristics in credit evaluation \citep{cfpb2026regb}. Legal obligations
are broader and fact dependent, so the experiment does not certify compliance.

\subsection{Counterfactual Pairs}

For each application, we change exactly one reported race, sex, or ethnicity
field and leave every financial document byte-identical. The policy label also
remains fixed. This is a metamorphic test: when an input that the sandbox
prohibits changes, the decision should not change. Similar behavioral testing
checks model capabilities through controlled input perturbations rather than
internal explanations \citep{ribeiro2020checklist}.

The pair does not estimate counterfactual fairness in the causal sense. It also
does not measure approval-rate disparity in the HMDA population. It answers a
smaller engineering question: did this workflow use a field that its supplied
policy explicitly excluded?

\subsection{Architectures}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\caption{Primary systems. Every role uses GPT-5.4-mini so architecture is the
manipulated factor.}
\label{tab:systems}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l c c Y@{}}
\toprule
System & Calls & Monitoring access & Mechanism \\
\midrule
Monolith & 1 & Full & One worker sees all evidence. \\
Plan-and-retrieve & 2 & Selected & A planner requests at most three evidence documents. \\
Committee & 4 & Segregated & Three parallel specialists report to a chair. \\
Guardrail & 3 & Audit only & A blind financial draft is audited, then revised. \\
Adaptive & 1 or 3 & Audit only & Only uncertain, non-approve, invalid, or missing-data drafts escalate. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The retrieval workflow follows the tool-selection idea behind reasoning-and-
acting systems \citep{yao2023react}. The committee resolves reports by policy
precedence, not majority vote. The guardrail separates financial underwriting
from demographic monitoring. Adaptive routing accepts only a valid approve
decision with confidence at least 0.90 and no missing-data marker; all other
drafts receive the same audit and revision as the fixed guardrail. A secondary
routing ablation separately compares always-primary, always-supervisor, and
selective-supervisor policies; its model effects are not attributed to
architecture.
